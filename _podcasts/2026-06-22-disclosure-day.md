<p>
Spielberg has done it again.
</p>

<p>
<a href="https://www.imdb.com/title/tt15047880/">Disclosure Day</a>
is the best film I've seen this year. Written by
<a href="https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0001429/">David Koepp</a>
and directed by
<a href="https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000229/">Steven Spielberg</a>,
this is a wide-eyed, optimistic, and deeply emotional sci-fi thriller
about first contact — and what it means for the people caught in the
middle of it. It is exactly the kind of movie only Spielberg could
make at this point in his career. A filmmaker writing his final
statement on the thing that shaped him.
</p>

<p>
<a href="https://www.imdb.com/name/nm1289434/">Emily Blunt</a>
as Margaret Fairchild is the heart of this film and she is perfectly
deployed — her likability and believability aren't incidental, they
are the whole point.
<a href="https://www.imdb.com/name/nm3725554/">Colman Domingo</a>
as Hugo is a walking bag of empathy and a reminder of what a special
talent he is.
<a href="https://www.imdb.com/name/nm3357337/">Josh O'Connor</a>
as Dr. Daniel Kellner anchors the film's scientific urgency, and
<a href="https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000147/">Colin Firth</a>
is genuinely chilling as a man who believes completely in the
rightness of what he is doing.
</p>

<p>
I saw this in IMAX with my wife. I've been thinking about it ever
since. Four and a half stars and a heart — and I mean every bit of it.
This one is special.
</p>
